The Tech Recruiting Timeline: A Brown Student’s Guide

Navigating the tech recruiting process can feel overwhelming, but doesn’t have to be. Use this timeline, created specifically for Brown University students, to understand what to expect each season, manage your job search effectively, and leverage resources from Brown’s Center for Career Exploration (CCE).

Late Summer (July–August)

What’s happening?

  • Early postings for next year’s internships and new grad roles, particularly from large tech companies: Summer2027-Internships, 2027-SWE-College-Jobs 
  • By mid-August, big-tech applications open widely.

Action Items:

  • Polish your resume & LinkedIn. Get a resume and LinkedIn profile review from a Peer Career Advisor (PCA).
  • Sharpen your interview skills. Depending on your targeted role, practice coding challenges, PM interview questions, or UX strategies using the Exponent platform. Create your personal interview prep timeline. We recommend 2-3 months of prep time for technical interviews if it’s your first time applying for tech internships.
  • Set job alerts. Subscribe to company career pages and job boards for timely postings.
  • Build or expand your professional network

Recruiting Tip: Applications are reviewed on a rolling basis, apply as soon as you see postings go live. You’ll have higher chances of getting an interview when your application is received early.

Early Fall (September–October)

What’s happening?

  • Peak recruiting season for tech internships and full-time roles, especially at large companies: Summer2027-Internships, 2027-SWE-College-Jobs 
  • Applications for most large tech companies close by the end of October.
  • Expect technical interviews and take-home assessments during this period.

Action Items:

  • Conduct mock interviews. Conduct mock interviews on Exponent with your peers or other job seekers, and keep practicing coding challenges. You should aim to get comfortable solving medium-grade problems without hints, and hard-grade problems with hints.
  • Balance your time. Allocate specific weekly hours for recruiting activities like networking and applying, and technical interview prep.
  • Customize applications. When you appropriately tailor your resume to each role, you stand a much larger chance of getting an interview.
  • Build or expand your professional network.

Recruiting Tip: Coding assessments are a window into your coding habits. Write clean, tested, and well-commented code.

Late Fall (November–December)

What’s happening?

  • Internship and full-time offers start rolling out.
  • Medium-sized companies and startups begin their recruiting efforts.
  • Recruiting significantly slows down between mid-December and mid-January due to the holidays, but picks back up right after.

Action Items

  • Keep applying. Plenty of jobs and internships continue to get posted by startups and mid-sized companies.
  • Evaluate and compare offers. Use CCE’s offer evaluation resources to assess roles, benefits, compensation, and growth opportunities.
  • Continue networking. Engage actively with medium-sized companies and startups through BrownConnect+ and LinkedIn to discover additional opportunities.

Recruiting Tip: Smaller companies often rely heavily on personal referrals, making networking even more essential.

Winter (January–February)

What’s happening?

  • Recruiting picks back up, especially among medium-sized companies and startups.
  • Big-tech recruiting for summer internships and new grad roles has largely ended.

Action Items

  • Actively network. Continue connecting with industry professionals and alumni to uncover roles at startups and mid-size firms.
  • Refine your pitch. Clearly articulate your interest and fit for opportunities at startups and smaller tech firms.

Recruiting Tip: Startups and smaller firms value versatility and enthusiasm, highlight relevant skills and projects prominently.

Spring (March–April)

What’s happening?

  • Final recruiting rounds for summer internships and remaining new grad roles.
  • Startups continue to post opportunities for graduating seniors and aspiring interns.

Action Items

  • Leverage spring projects. Highlight academic or side projects on your resume, GitHub, and in interviews.
  • Actively network. Continue connecting with industry professionals and alumni to uncover roles at startups.
  • Prepare for the next recruiting cycle. Identify summer opportunities that build your experience and strengthen your profile.

Recruiting Tip: Seniors still looking for full-time roles should actively monitor postings at startups and leverage their networks.

Early Summer (May–June)

What’s happening?

  • New grads and interns start roles.
  • Companies plan hiring needs for the coming year, and the recruiting cycle resets by July.
  • Startups continue to hire for new-grad full-time roles

Action Items

  • Onboard successfully. Learn how to succeed in new roles and internships.
  • Reflect and strategize. Assess your recruiting experience, identify successes and areas for improvement for the next cycle. Speak with a mentor on BrownConnect+.
  • Still looking for a new-grad role? Continue connecting with industry professionals and alumni to uncover roles at startups.

Recruiting Tip: Use this quieter period to organize yourself for the next recruiting season, update resumes, enhance your LinkedIn profile, and set early application reminders.

Essential Brown Resources

  • Peer Career Advisors (PCAs): Come to open hours, no appointment required!
  • BrownConnect+: Network with alumni and explore Bruno Opportunities.
  • Exponent Interview Prep: Free premium access for all Brown students.

Keep this timeline handy, stay proactive, and reach out to CCE anytime you need support. You've got this!
